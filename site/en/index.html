01 About

I’ve lived several professional lives — and every one of them started with a leap.

I founded my first company, Information Delivery Systems, a Business Intelligence consulting boutique, while I was still at university. Then I grew Concept in Milan for over a decade, turning consulting into software products, such as myK, listed by Gartner among the leading Enterprise Feedback Management solutions — and co-founded Couponline, a joint venture that brought couponing to large-scale retail with a new twist: coupons delivered at store check-in, rather than printed at checkout like the incumbents. Then I left the comfort zone of the place I was born and raised and moved to San Francisco, where I launched and led Opinionage, the social network of opinions, and Thingle.me, the Customer Support of Things, as CEO — raising capital, building teams and shipping products on both sides of the Atlantic.

Along the way I was featured in Starting-Up in America (2011), a documentary by Tarik Ansari and Basil Glew-Galloway on the challenges international entrepreneurs face with US immigration to build their companies in Silicon Valley.

In 2021 I made another leap — from entrepreneur to intrapreneur — joining PagoPA’s Strategic Innovation department. Today I’m Product Lead at PagoPA, the state-controlled company behind Italy’s public digital payments and services platforms. There we launched the Public Administration’s first developer portal, now used by thousands of public bodies and technology partners, and its AI assistant grew into DOS68K, an open-source RAG platform.

What ties it together: I’ve been building things since I rigged a brake light onto my bicycle at ten out of light bulbs, wires, batteries, tape and a lot of imagination, and every change of skin has made me a lifelong learner. I’m fascinated by cognitive science — Hofstadter and Dennett in particular, starting with the books that truly shaped me: The Mind’s I and Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid (GEB to its friends).

02 How I work

01

Zero to one

I’m at my best when a product doesn’t exist yet. I look far ahead, beyond what’s already there: doing the same thing twice bores me, and my ground is 0→1 more than scale-up.

02

The best technology is the kind you don’t see

Coupons handed out at the store entrance, support through a QR code, a butler without screens: a good product removes friction instead of adding interfaces.

03

Quick, not dirty

Fast, yes, but every step has to become solid ground for the next one. That’s why I prototype before putting my hands on the real product: I don’t chase demos, I chase what works every day.

04

Build to understand

I stay close to the code and put ideas to the test of real-world constraints: if they hold there, they hold anywhere.

05

Trust first, then autonomy

Especially with AI: privacy by design, local processing whenever possible, autonomy only where it is reliable and controllable.

06

Small teams, clear ownership

Small “two-pizza” teams rather than over-structured processes. The best ideas come from cross-pollination between disciplines, but diffused responsibility doesn’t get you far. I look for proactive people who bring solutions and don’t get discouraged by challenges.
